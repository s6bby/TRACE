PURPOSE: A Behavioral Intervention Plan (BIP) is a plan incorporated into the student’s IEP if determined necessary by the IEP team for the student to receive FAPE (WAC 392-172A-01031). The IEP team must also develop and implement a BIP if it determines that the student’s conduct is a manifestation of the student’s disability, unless a BIP is already in place (WAC 392-172A-05147).

Pattern of Behavior (describe the pattern of behavior(s) that impedes the student’s learning or the learning of others):
Student engages in aggressive and disruptive behaviors during transitions and when denied access to preferred activities. Behaviors include: | Yelling | Verbal statements (“I hate you,” “I am never coming to school again”) | Hitting peers | Staff-directed aggression | Throwing body against furniture | Frequency: 3–5 incidents per week, with some weeks occurring daily. | Severity ranges from verbal disruption to physical aggression toward peers or staff. | Behavior interferes with academic engagement and peer relationships.
Instructional and Environmental Conditions or Circumstances (describe the instructional and environmental conditions or circumstances that trigger/contribute to the pattern of behavior(s) being addressed):
The pattern of behavior is most likely to occur when: | Transitioning from preferred to non-preferred tasks | Denied access to tablet, fish-related content, or food reinforcement | Required to participate in peer-selected activities not aligned with interests | Engaging in multi-step academic tasks requiring sustained effort | Physical fatigue increases during longer instructional blocks | Behavior is hypothesized to function as: | Escape from non-preferred academic or social demands | Access to preferred activities or adult attention
Alternative Behaviors and Skills to be Taught and Monitored (describe the alternative behaviors and skills to be taught and monitored in order to address the pattern of behaviors and the instructional and environmental conditions/ circumstances previously described):
Student will be explicitly taught and monitored on the following replacement behaviors: | Appropriately requesting a break using verbal language or break card. | Requesting help when frustrated. | Using coping strategies (deep breathing, counting, squeezing sensory tool). | Accepting “no” or delayed access with visual timer support. | Transitioning within 1 prompt without physical aggression. | Instruction will occur during: | Behavioral instruction minutes (60 minutes/week) | Daily classroom practice with adult prompting | OT sessions supporting regulation strategies
Reinforcers of Desired Behaviors (describe how the alternative behaviors will be reinforced):
Desired behaviors will be reinforced using: | Immediate specific verbal praise | Fish-themed token system (5 tokens = 5 minutes fish drawing/video) | Access to preferred coloring activity | Positive note home | Small edible reinforcer when appropriate (as pre-approved by parent) | Reinforcement will be delivered on a frequent schedule initially and faded gradually as independence increases.
Methods to Ensure Consistency of Implementation (describe the method(s) that will be used to ensure the consistency of the implementation of the positive behavioral interventions across the student’s school-sponsored instruction or activities (e.g., data collection, frequency, assessment of effectiveness of BIP, additional training for staff, etc.)):
Behavior frequency data collected daily by classroom staff. | Weekly review of behavior data by case manager and BCBA. | Quarterly progress review aligned with IEP progress reports. | Staff training provided on: | Transition supports | Prompt hierarchy | Reinforcement delivery | All staff working with student provided a copy of the BIP. | BIP effectiveness formally reviewed at least every 6 weeks. | Adjustments made based on data trends.
