Pattern of Behavior (describe the pattern of behavior(s) that impedes the student’s learning or the learning of others):
Noah demonstrates a persistent pattern of behavioral dysregulation during transitions and non-preferred activities that impedes his access to kindergarten instruction. Specifically: (1) Crying and falling to the floor during activity transitions, occurring 3–5 times per school day; (2) Refusing to leave a preferred activity when prompted (staying seated, saying “no,” pushing adult hand away), occurring at approximately 60% of transition points; (3) Brief tantrum behaviors (dropping materials, hitting table surface) during transitions or when requests are denied, occurring 2–3 times per day; (4) Crying during fine motor tasks perceived as frustrating (cutting, writing), occurring 1–2 times per instructional period. Behaviors are most frequent at activity transitions, particularly from preferred play or movement activities to structured seat work. Average duration with adult co-regulation is 3–5 minutes. Function is assessed as escape from transitions and access to continuation of preferred activities.
Instructional and Environmental Conditions or Circumstances (describe the instructional and environmental conditions or circumstances that trigger/contribute to the pattern of behavior(s) being addressed):
Transition-related dysregulation is most frequent under the following conditions: (1) When transitioning away from preferred sensory or play activities (e.g., sensory bin, outdoor play, building blocks) to structured academic or fine motor tasks; (2) When transitions occur without advance warning (abrupt redirection by adult); (3) During the first and last 30 minutes of the school day, when Noah is most fatigued or has not yet regulated from arrival; (4) During fine motor tasks (cutting, writing) when the task is perceived as too difficult or when Noah does not have a clear model or visual support. Antecedent FBA data indicate that advance visual and verbal warnings reduce transition-related dysregulation by approximately 50%. Behaviors are rare during preferred sensory activities and music. Function is assessed as escape from transitions perceived as abrupt and access to continuation of preferred activities.
Alternative Behaviors and Skills to be Taught and Monitored (describe the alternative behaviors and skills to be taught and monitored in order to address the pattern of behaviors and the instructional and environmental conditions/ circumstances previously described):
The following alternative behaviors and skills will be explicitly taught and monitored: (1) Transition Warning Response: Noah will be explicitly taught to respond to the visual countdown (2-minute and 1-minute picture timer) as a cue that a transition is coming. He will be taught to verbalize or sign “almost done” when the timer appears, and then to stand up and move when the timer ends. This will be practiced with positive reinforcement during low-stakes transitions before being applied to challenging ones. (2) First-Then Board Routine: Noah will use a First-Then board (picture symbols) posted at his workspace. Before non-preferred or challenging activities, staff will show Noah the First-Then board (“First writing, then sensory bin”) to establish predictability and a concrete reward for completion. (3) Emotion Identification: Noah will be taught to identify and label “happy,” “sad,” “mad,” and “frustrated” using a simple 4-picture feelings chart. He will be prompted to point to or name his feeling before and after transitions during structured teaching opportunities. (4) Break Request: Noah will be taught to use a picture break card (handed to adult) to request a 2-minute sensory break when he is frustrated, rather than crying or falling to the floor. Staff will respond to the break card immediately and model return-to-task behavior following the break. (5) Fine Motor Access Strategy: Before fine motor tasks, Noah will be provided a visual model of the task and a reduced-demand version (shorter cutting line, fewer letters to trace) to reduce the perceived difficulty that triggers task-related crying.
Reinforcers of Desired Behaviors (describe how the alternative behaviors will be reinforced):
Desired behaviors will be reinforced using the following strategies, selected based on Noah’s identified motivators (sensory bin, trains, outdoor play, music, and one-on-one adult attention): (1) Behavior-Specific Praise: Staff will provide immediate, enthusiastic specific praise when Noah uses his break card, responds to the transition timer, or completes a transition without crying (e.g., “Noah, you came right over when the timer went off! Great job!”). Praise will be delivered immediately, warmly, and at eye level. (2) First-Then Reward Access: Upon completing a non-preferred task or transition using the First-Then structure, Noah will immediately access the “Then” activity (sensory bin, trains, music). The reward must follow the target behavior within 60 seconds to be effective given Noah’s developmental level. (3) Token Chart: Noah will use a simple 5-token smiley face chart. Each successful transition with the timer earns one token. Upon filling the chart, Noah earns a preferred activity of his choice (outdoor play, sensory bin, trains). The chart is visual and concrete to match his developmental level. (4) Transition Celebrations: For especially challenging transitions (e.g., end of outdoor play), staff will briefly co-celebrate with Noah immediately after a successful transition (“You did it! High five! Now let’s do writing together”) to build a positive emotional association with the transition behavior.
Methods to Ensure Consistency of Implementation (describe the method(s) that will be used to ensure the consistency of the implementation of the positive behavioral interventions across the student’s school-sponsored instruction or activities (e.g., data collection, frequency, assessment of effectiveness of BIP, additional training for staff, etc.)):
The following methods will be used to ensure consistent implementation of the BIP across all school-sponsored instruction and activities: (1) Data Collection: The special education teacher or paraeducator will collect a daily frequency tally of transition-related dysregulation behaviors (crying, floor dropping, refusal) and successful transition uses (timer response with no tantrum) across all transitions in the school day. Break card uses and First-Then completions will also be tallied daily. Data will be reviewed weekly and graphed monthly for IEP team review. (2) Staff Communication and Training: All school staff who work with Noah (classroom teacher, paraeducator, SLP, OT, specials teachers, recess/lunch supervisors) will receive a copy of the BIP and attend a 30-minute orientation. Training will include: how to use the visual timer, First-Then board protocol, break card response procedure, and behavior-specific praise delivery. The BIP will be reviewed monthly during IEP team check-ins. (3) Assessment of BIP Effectiveness: Transition success rate and dysregulation frequency data will be graphed monthly. The team will compare data to baseline (60% tantrum rate at transitions) to evaluate progress toward IEP Goal #4 (transition regulation). If no measurable improvement is observed within 6 weeks, the team will convene to review the BIP and consider adjustments to the warning timing, reward menu, or request for an additional FBA. (4) Parent Communication: Parents will receive a copy of the BIP and will be informed of Noah’s progress through quarterly written reports. Staff will contact the family same-day for any injury-related incidents. Home-school consistency around First-Then routines and transition warnings will be encouraged; parent coaching sessions will be offered through the SLP or special education teacher.
