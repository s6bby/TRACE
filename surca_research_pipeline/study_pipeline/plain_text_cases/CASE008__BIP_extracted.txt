Pattern of Behavior (describe the pattern of behavior(s) that impedes the student’s learning or the learning of others):
Aisha demonstrates a persistent pattern of withdrawal and disengagement during verbal-heavy instruction that impedes her access to grade-level content. Specifically: (1) Passive disengagement (appearing attentive but missing content, not recording notes, not following along) occurring 3–4 times per class period during extended verbal instruction without captioning; (2) Non-participation in class discussions and group activities (not raising hand, one-word or no responses when called on) occurring 3–5 times per class period; (3) Appearing to have completed tasks but answering incorrectly due to missed verbal instructions, occurring 2–3 times per week; (4) Reluctance to self-advocate (request repetition, adjust FM system, request captioning) when auditory access is poor, occurring in approximately 80% of such opportunities. These behaviors are directly linked to auditory accessibility; they do not occur when full CART captioning and FM system are in use. Function is assessed as avoidance of the social vulnerability of repeatedly asking for repetition in front of peers.
Instructional and Environmental Conditions or Circumstances (describe the instructional and environmental conditions or circumstances that trigger/contribute to the pattern of behavior(s) being addressed):
Disengagement and non-advocacy behaviors are most frequent under the following conditions: (1) During whole-class verbal instruction without CART captioning, particularly during fast-paced discussions, teacher lecture, and instructional videos without captions; (2) During group activities where multiple people speak at once, making lip-reading and FM use ineffective; (3) When seated in positions where the speaker's face is obscured or Aisha is more than 6 feet from the primary speaker without FM microphone relay; (4) In noisy classroom environments (e.g., open-plan classes, cafeteria-adjacent rooms, after specials transitions) where background noise competes with the FM signal. Antecedent FBA data indicate that nearly all disengagement behaviors occur when CART is not active or FM signal quality is poor. Behaviors are absent in 1:1 instruction, small groups with visual support, and classes with consistent CART coverage. Function is assessed as avoidance of the social cost of repeatedly requesting accommodations in front of hearing peers.
Alternative Behaviors and Skills to be Taught and Monitored (describe the alternative behaviors and skills to be taught and monitored in order to address the pattern of behaviors and the instructional and environmental conditions/ circumstances previously described):
The following alternative behaviors and skills will be explicitly taught and monitored: (1) Self-Advocacy Request Procedure: Aisha will be directly taught and rehearsed a set of specific, low-social-cost self-advocacy phrases and signals (e.g., a discrete hand signal to request repetition; a written note card to pass to the teacher requesting caption replay) to use when she has missed auditory content, reducing the social visibility of the request. Staff will respond to all self-advocacy signals within 30 seconds without drawing class attention to the request. (2) FM System Setup and Monitoring Routine: Aisha will be taught to independently check FM connectivity, signal quality, and microphone placement at the start of each class period using a brief 2-step checklist at her desk. She will be taught to alert the teacher or paraeducator if signal quality is poor before instruction begins. (3) Listening Fatigue Recognition: Aisha will be explicitly taught to identify her own signs of listening fatigue (increased inattention, headache, difficulty following) and to request a 2-minute quiet break or shift to a visual task when these signs appear, rather than disengaging silently. (4) Caption Review Habit: Aisha will be taught to use CART caption history or class notes to verify key content she may have missed at natural break points in instruction (e.g., end of a lesson segment), rather than proceeding with incomplete information. (5) Peer Communication Protocol: Aisha and her teacher will collaboratively develop a low-stakes system for group activities (e.g., a designated peer note-taker, a visual turn-taking signal) that reduces the auditory processing demand of simultaneous group conversation.
Reinforcers of Desired Behaviors (describe how the alternative behaviors will be reinforced):
Desired behaviors will be reinforced using the following strategies, selected based on Aisha’s identified motivators (art projects, technology, peer recognition, and adult affirmation): (1) Behavior-Specific Praise: Staff will provide immediate, private praise (written note or brief private verbal) when Aisha uses a self-advocacy signal, checks FM quality, or requests repetition (e.g., a brief written sticky note: “Great job using your signal today”). Public praise is avoided to prevent drawing attention to hearing-related accommodations. (2) Self-Advocacy Tracking: Aisha will maintain a private weekly log of self-advocacy uses (any signal, FM check, or content clarification request). When she achieves 10 entries in a week, she earns a preferred activity (e.g., art project time, digital storytelling choice, tech exploration). (3) Competence-Building Framing: Staff and the DHH specialist will regularly affirm Aisha’s self-advocacy as a professional and academic strength (“Students who advocate for their access needs succeed in college and careers”). This reframes the act of requesting accommodations from a social liability to a skill to be proud of. (4) Access Quality as Reward: When Aisha proactively ensures her FM and CART are working at the start of class, she is set up for full instructional access—which itself functions as a natural reinforcer, as she can engage more fully with peers and content she enjoys.
Methods to Ensure Consistency of Implementation (describe the method(s) that will be used to ensure the consistency of the implementation of the positive behavioral interventions across the student’s school-sponsored instruction or activities (e.g., data collection, frequency, assessment of effectiveness of BIP, additional training for staff, etc.)):
The following methods will be used to ensure consistent implementation of the BIP across all school-sponsored instruction and activities: (1) Data Collection: The DHH specialist and general education teacher will collect daily data on FM system use, CART activation rate, and self-advocacy signal uses per class period using a structured checklist. Disengagement observations (passive non-participation, missed content) will be recorded by the paraeducator or DHH specialist during bi-weekly interval observations. Data will be reviewed weekly and graphed monthly. (2) Staff Communication and Training: All instructional staff who work with Aisha across content areas will receive a copy of the BIP and attend a 30-minute orientation prior to implementation. Training will include: how to pass the FM microphone, how to activate CART at session start, appropriate response to self-advocacy signals, and how to facilitate group activities with a note-taker structure. The BIP will be reviewed monthly during IEP team check-ins. (3) Assessment of BIP Effectiveness: Self-advocacy frequency and disengagement observation data will be graphed monthly. The team will compare data to baseline to evaluate progress toward IEP Goal #2 (listening/communication access). If self-advocacy rates do not improve within 6 weeks of consistent implementation, the team will convene to consider additional support, including counseling referral to address social stigma around hearing loss. (4) Parent Communication: Parents will receive a copy of the BIP and will be informed of Aisha’s progress through quarterly written reports. Staff will contact the family if disengagement increases or if FM/CART equipment issues are identified. Parent input on Aisha’s home communication preferences and self-advocacy comfort level will be solicited quarterly.
