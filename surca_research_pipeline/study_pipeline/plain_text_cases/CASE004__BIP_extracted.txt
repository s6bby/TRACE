Pattern of Behavior (describe the pattern of behavior(s) that impedes the student’s learning or the learning of others):
Sofia demonstrates a persistent pattern of emotional dysregulation and physical aggression that impedes her learning and the learning of classmates. Specifically: (1) Behavioral escalations (screaming, crying, throwing or pushing materials, and physical aggression toward peers or staff) occurring 3–4 times per school day on average (baseline observation, February 2026); (2) Physical aggression toward peers (hitting, kicking, pushing) occurring 4–5 times per week; (3) Verbal aggression toward staff or peers (name-calling, threats) occurring daily; (4) Refusal to transition between activities or locations, occurring at approximately 60% of all transition points. Escalations last an average of 10–15 minutes and require significant adult intervention. These behaviors occur most frequently during transitions, recess, and unstructured periods, but also occur during academic tasks that are perceived as difficult or unfair.
Instructional and Environmental Conditions or Circumstances (describe the instructional and environmental conditions or circumstances that trigger/contribute to the pattern of behavior(s) being addressed):
Behavioral escalations are most frequent under the following conditions: (1) During transitions between activities or locations, particularly unannounced or abrupt transitions; (2) During unstructured periods (recess, lunch) when adult supervision is less direct and peer conflicts are more likely; (3) When a preferred activity is ended or a request is denied; (4) When adult attention is diverted to another student during independent work or group instruction. Antecedent FBA data indicate that approximately 60% of escalations are immediately preceded by a transition demand and approximately 30% follow a denied request. Escalations are significantly less frequent during 1:1 or small-group instruction with a preferred adult. Function of behaviors is assessed as escape from demands (transitions) and access to adult attention.
Alternative Behaviors and Skills to be Taught and Monitored (describe the alternative behaviors and skills to be taught and monitored in order to address the pattern of behaviors and the instructional and environmental conditions/ circumstances previously described):
The following alternative behaviors and skills will be explicitly taught and monitored: (1) Break Request Procedure: Sofia will be directly taught and rehearsed the skill of using a break card placed on her desk to request a 3-minute calm-down break before escalation, rather than engaging in aggression or refusal. This will be modeled, role-played, and cued with a visual prompt. Teacher will acknowledge the break request within 30 seconds and provide access to the designated calm-down corner. (2) Emotion Identification and Labeling: Sofia will be explicitly taught to identify and label her emotional states using a 5-point feelings scale (Zones of Regulation). She will practice checking in with her feelings scale 3 times per day (morning, after lunch, mid-afternoon). (3) Transition Warning Protocol: Sofia will receive a 5-minute and 2-minute visual and verbal transition warning before all activity changes. She will be taught and rehearsed to use the verbal phrase “I’m not ready yet” to request a 1-minute additional wait before transitioning, rather than refusing entirely. (4) Calm-Down Strategy Menu: Sofia will select 2 calming strategies from a taught menu (deep breathing, squeeze ball, draw one picture, count to 10) to use during her break or when prompted by staff. Selected strategies will be posted at her calm-down corner. (5) Conflict Resolution Script: During regulated periods, Sofia will be taught and practiced a simple peer conflict script (“Stop. I don’t like that. Walk away.”) for use when peer provocation occurs during recess or unstructured time.
Reinforcers of Desired Behaviors (describe how the alternative behaviors will be reinforced):
Desired behaviors will be reinforced using the following strategies, selected based on Sofia’s identified motivators (adult praise, animal-themed activities, art activities, and special classroom jobs): (1) Behavior-Specific Praise: Staff will provide immediate, specific verbal praise when Sofia uses a break card, completes a transition, or uses a calming strategy (e.g., “Sofia, you asked for a break and then came right back — that was a great choice”). Praise will be delivered calmly and privately to avoid overstimulation. (2) Token Economy: Sofia will earn stickers on a visual chart for each successful transition and each break card use. Upon earning 5 stickers, she may select a reward from a preference menu (e.g., 5 minutes of drawing, being the line leader, or choosing a classroom job for the day). (3) Check-In/Check-Out System: Sofia will participate in a daily check-in/check-out system with her identified adult anchor (special education teacher). Morning check-in sets the day’s goal; afternoon check-out reviews progress and provides positive feedback. This daily connection functions as a reinforcer by meeting Sofia’s attention needs through a proactive, structured channel. (4) Class Privilege for Regulation Goals: On days when Sofia meets her daily regulation goal, she earns access to a preferred activity during the final 10 minutes of the school day.
Methods to Ensure Consistency of Implementation (describe the method(s) that will be used to ensure the consistency of the implementation of the positive behavioral interventions across the student’s school-sponsored instruction or activities (e.g., data collection, frequency, assessment of effectiveness of BIP, additional training for staff, etc.)):
The following methods will be used to ensure consistent implementation of the BIP across all school-sponsored instruction and activities: (1) Data Collection: The special education teacher or paraeducator will collect daily frequency data on behavioral escalations, physical aggression incidents, and successful break card uses using a structured tally sheet. Transition success rate (number of successful transitions out of total daily transitions) will be tracked daily. Check-in/check-out data will be reviewed weekly to monitor trends. (2) Staff Communication and Training: All instructional staff who work with Sofia (general education teacher, special education teacher, paraeducator, recess/lunch supervisors) will receive a copy of the BIP and participate in a 45-minute orientation prior to implementation, including practice of the transition warning protocol and de-escalation language. The BIP will be reviewed at monthly team meetings to assess fidelity and adjust procedures as needed. (3) Assessment of BIP Effectiveness: Incident frequency data and transition success rates will be graphed monthly and reviewed by the IEP team. The team will compare current data to baseline to evaluate progress toward IEP Goal #1 (reduce escalations) and Goal #2 (self-regulation strategy use). If no measurable improvement is observed within 6 weeks of consistent implementation, the team will convene to review and revise the BIP, including potential referral for additional FBA. (4) Parent Communication: Parents will receive a copy of the BIP and will be informed of Sofia’s progress through quarterly written reports. Staff will contact the family same-day for any incidents involving physical injury or significant property damage, and will provide weekly brief phone or email updates during the first month of implementation.
