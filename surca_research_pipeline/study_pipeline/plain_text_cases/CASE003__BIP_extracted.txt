Pattern of Behavior (describe the pattern of behavior(s) that impedes the student’s learning or the learning of others):
Ethan demonstrates a persistent pattern of avoidance and task-refusal behavior during literacy-demanding tasks that impedes his learning and the learning of others. Specifically: (1) Avoidance behaviors (requesting restroom, putting head down, sharpening pencils, making off-topic comments) occurring 4–6 times per 45-minute ELA period; (2) Task refusal on independent reading or written expression assignments, occurring 2–3 times per week; (3) Low on-task rate during independent ELA work (approximately 40%, observed January 2026); (4) Verbal statements of helplessness during literacy tasks (e.g., “I can’t do this,” “This is too hard”) occurring 2–4 times per ELA period. These behaviors occur primarily during ELA instruction and content-area assignments requiring independent reading or written responses. Peer interactions are generally positive and conflicts are infrequent outside ELA blocks.
Instructional and Environmental Conditions or Circumstances (describe the instructional and environmental conditions or circumstances that trigger/contribute to the pattern of behavior(s) being addressed):
Avoidance and refusal behaviors are most frequent under the following conditions: (1) During independent ELA work requiring sustained decoding, reading comprehension, or written expression; (2) When assignments involve reading multi-paragraph texts without support (e.g., text-to-speech, oral read-aloud); (3) During timed writing tasks or assignments with perceived high stakes (tests, grade-level writing prompts); (4) When the student has not been given a clear entry point or structured scaffold for the task. Antecedent FBA data indicate that avoidance behaviors escalate significantly within the first 5 minutes of an independent ELA task. Verbal statements of helplessness peak during tasks that emphasize decoding or spelling without support. Function of behaviors is assessed as escape from literacy tasks perceived as impossible or humiliating. Behaviors are absent or minimal during math, science, and cooperative learning activities with low reading demands.
Alternative Behaviors and Skills to be Taught and Monitored (describe the alternative behaviors and skills to be taught and monitored in order to address the pattern of behaviors and the instructional and environmental conditions/ circumstances previously described):
The following alternative behaviors and skills will be explicitly taught and monitored: (1) Help-Requesting Skill: Ethan will be directly taught and rehearsed the skill of raising his hand or placing a “help” card on his desk to request adult support when a task feels overwhelming, rather than engaging in avoidance. This will be modeled, practiced, and cued with a visual prompt at his workspace. (2) Task Initiation Strategy: Ethan will be taught a “PASS” strategy (Pause, Ask one question, Start small, Stay for 5 minutes) to support initiation of difficult literacy tasks without avoidance. This strategy will be rehearsed during pull-out sessions and prompted by staff during ELA. (3) Self-Monitoring Form: Ethan will use a brief daily self-monitoring form during ELA work to rate his engagement level and note one strategy he used. This form will be reviewed with the special education teacher at end of day. (4) Reframing Negative Self-Talk: Ethan will be explicitly taught to identify and challenge negative academic self-talk (e.g., “I can’t do this” → “This is hard but I can try the first part”). Scripts will be practiced during social-emotional learning check-ins. (5) Structured Access to Accommodations: Ethan will be trained to independently activate text-to-speech software and graphic organizers before beginning literacy tasks to reduce the difficulty barrier that triggers avoidance.
Reinforcers of Desired Behaviors (describe how the alternative behaviors will be reinforced):
Desired behaviors will be reinforced using the following strategies, selected based on Ethan’s identified motivators (science activities, peer collaboration, teacher recognition, and choice-based tasks): (1) Behavior-Specific Praise: Staff will provide immediate, specific verbal praise when Ethan initiates a literacy task, uses a help card, or activates accommodations independently (e.g., “Ethan, I saw you use the help card and then get started — that takes real persistence”). Praise will emphasize effort and strategy use rather than product quality. (2) Task Completion Reward: When Ethan completes a full ELA independent work period with two or fewer avoidance behaviors, he earns a point on a visual progress tracker. Upon accumulating 5 points, he may choose a reward from a preference menu (e.g., 10 minutes of science exploration, choosing a partner for the next project, or sharing a science fact with the class). (3) Competence-Building Recognition: Weekly, the special education teacher will share one example of Ethan’s reading or writing progress with Ethan privately, framing it in terms of measurable growth to support academic self-concept. (4) Accommodation Access as Positive Support: Access to text-to-speech and graphic organizers is framed as a tool of strength, not a deficit accommodation, to reduce stigma and increase willingness to engage with literacy tasks.
Methods to Ensure Consistency of Implementation (describe the method(s) that will be used to ensure the consistency of the implementation of the positive behavioral interventions across the student’s school-sponsored instruction or activities (e.g., data collection, frequency, assessment of effectiveness of BIP, additional training for staff, etc.)):
The following methods will be used to ensure consistent implementation of the BIP across all school-sponsored instruction and activities: (1) Data Collection: The special education teacher will collect frequency data on avoidance behaviors and task refusal incidents using a daily tally sheet during all ELA instructional periods. Interval observation data for on-task behavior during ELA will be collected by the special education teacher or a designated paraeducator a minimum of twice per week using a 5-minute partial interval recording system. Ethan’s daily self-monitoring form will be reviewed weekly to evaluate accuracy and trends. (2) Staff Communication and Training: All instructional staff who work with Ethan (general education teacher, special education teacher, paraeducator) will receive a copy of the BIP and attend a 30-minute orientation meeting prior to implementation. Staff will also receive brief training on the text-to-speech and graphic organizer tools to facilitate consistent access across settings. The BIP will be reviewed monthly during team check-ins to assess fidelity. (3) Assessment of BIP Effectiveness: Avoidance behavior and on-task rate data will be graphed monthly and reviewed by the IEP team. The team will compare current data to baseline to evaluate progress toward IEP Goal #4 (task engagement during ELA). If no measurable reduction in avoidance behaviors is observed within 6 weeks of consistent implementation, the team will convene to review and revise the BIP. (4) Parent Communication: Parents will receive a copy of the BIP and will be informed of Ethan’s behavioral progress through quarterly written reports. Staff will contact the family if avoidance behaviors escalate significantly from baseline levels or if a new concern arises.
