Pattern of Behavior (describe the pattern of behavior(s) that impedes the student’s learning or the learning of others):
Maya demonstrates a persistent pattern of off-task and disruptive behavior that impedes her learning and the learning of classmates. Specifically: (1) Calling out without raising hand or being called upon, occurring 8–10 times per 45-minute instructional period; (2) Leaving her seat without permission 2–3 times per period; (3) Difficulty sustaining attention during independent work, resulting in on-task rates of approximately 45% (observed February 2026); (4) Interrupting instruction and peer work time with comments, questions, and physical movements. These behaviors occur across academic subjects but are most frequent during independent reading and writing tasks and during less structured activities. Peer conflicts arise from impulsive physical contact and verbal interruptions, occurring 2–3 times per week.
Instructional and Environmental Conditions or Circumstances (describe the instructional and environmental conditions or circumstances that trigger/contribute to the pattern of behavior(s) being addressed):
Behaviors are most frequent and intense under the following conditions: (1) During independent academic work periods, particularly reading comprehension assignments and written expression tasks that require sustained mental effort for 15 or more minutes; (2) During large-group instruction when Maya is expected to listen without frequent active participation; (3) In less structured transitions or free-choice periods where expectations are ambiguous; (4) When work is perceived as too difficult or lengthy without clear chunking or checkpoints. Antecedent FBA data indicate that approximately 70% of calling-out incidents occur within the first 10 minutes of an independent work task. Peer conflicts are typically preceded by close physical proximity in cooperative group settings without assigned roles. Function of behaviors is assessed as primarily attention-seeking (calling out, getting out of seat) and escape from non-preferred tasks (off-task behavior, frequent breaks without permission).
Alternative Behaviors and Skills to be Taught and Monitored (describe the alternative behaviors and skills to be taught and monitored in order to address the pattern of behaviors and the instructional and environmental conditions/ circumstances previously described):
The following alternative behaviors and skills will be explicitly taught and monitored: (1) Raise-Hand Protocol: Maya will be directly taught and practiced the skill of raising her hand and waiting silently to be called on before speaking during instruction. This will be modeled, role-played, and rehearsed in multiple settings. Teacher will use a visual prompt card on Maya’s desk as a reminder. (2) Self-Monitoring Checklist: Maya will use a structured self-monitoring form during independent work periods to track her on-task behavior at 5-minute intervals. She will rate herself using a 3-point scale (0=off task, 1=somewhat on task, 2=fully on task). (3) Break Request Procedure: Maya will be taught a structured break-request procedure using a break card. She is permitted to request one scheduled break per 45-minute period by placing her break card on the corner of her desk. The teacher will acknowledge within 30 seconds and provide a 3-minute movement break at an appropriate stopping point. (4) Work Initiation Strategy: Maya will be taught a “STAR” self-regulation strategy (Stop, Think, Act, Review) to support independent task initiation and self-checking. (5) Peer Interaction Skills: In cooperative group activities, Maya will practice assigned-role participation and using a quiet voice to direct comments to peers.
Reinforcers of Desired Behaviors (describe how the alternative behaviors will be reinforced):
Desired behaviors will be reinforced using the following strategies, selected based on Maya’s identified motivators (adult attention, art activities, peer recognition, and movement): (1) Behavior-Specific Praise: Staff will provide immediate, specific verbal praise when Maya raises her hand, stays on task, or uses the break request procedure correctly (e.g., “Maya, I noticed you raised your hand and waited — great job showing self-control”). Praise will be delivered on a variable ratio schedule to maintain the behavior over time. (2) Self-Monitoring Match Reward: At the end of each independent work period, Maya will compare her self-monitoring rating with the teacher’s rating. If her scores match within 1 point, she earns a token. Tokens are accumulated toward a choice reward menu including 5 minutes of art time, a special helper role, or choosing the class read-aloud. (3) Class-Wide Reinforcement Tie-In: Maya’s participation in the class’s behavior chart (whole-class marble jar) is maintained. When Maya demonstrates target behaviors, the class is acknowledged collectively, providing natural peer-level positive social feedback. (4) Movement Break as Scheduled Reinforcer: The structured movement break itself functions as a reinforcer for appropriate task engagement; Maya earns the break by remaining on task until the designated break point rather than receiving it contingent on escape behaviors.
Methods to Ensure Consistency of Implementation (describe the method(s) that will be used to ensure the consistency of the implementation of the positive behavioral interventions across the student’s school-sponsored instruction or activities (e.g., data collection, frequency, assessment of effectiveness of BIP, additional training for staff, etc.)):
The following methods will be used to ensure consistent implementation of the BIP across all school-sponsored instruction and activities: (1) Data Collection: The special education teacher will collect frequency data on calling-out incidents and out-of-seat behavior using a daily tally sheet during all instructional periods. Interval observation data for on-task behavior will be collected by the special education teacher or a designated paraeducator a minimum of twice per week using a 5-minute partial interval recording system. The self-monitoring checklist data will be collected daily and reviewed by the teacher weekly. (2) Staff Communication and Training: All instructional staff who work with Maya (general education teacher, special education teacher, paraeducator) will receive a copy of the BIP and will participate in a 30-minute orientation meeting prior to implementation. The BIP will be reviewed monthly during team check-ins to assess fidelity and effectiveness. (3) Assessment of BIP Effectiveness: Data will be graphed monthly and reviewed by the IEP team. The team will compare current data to baseline to evaluate progress toward IEP Goal #1 (on-task behavior) and Goal #2 (self-regulated communication). If no measurable improvement is observed within 6 weeks of consistent implementation, the team will convene to review and revise the BIP. (4) Parent Communication: Parents will receive a copy of the BIP and will be informed of Maya’s progress toward behavioral goals through quarterly written reports. Staff will contact parents if behaviors escalate significantly from baseline or if a new concern arises.
