Pattern of Behavior (describe the pattern of behavior(s) that impedes the student’s learning or the learning of others):
Destiny demonstrates a persistent pattern of avoidance and withdrawal during verbal academic tasks that impedes her participation and learning. Specifically: (1) Non-response when called on in whole-class settings, occurring 4–5 times per ELA class period; (2) Minimal verbal responses (one-word answers, shrugging, head-down) during open-ended oral questions, occurring daily; (3) Requesting to leave the room (restroom, nurse) during oral reading or discussion activities, occurring 2–3 times per week; (4) Declining to read aloud or respond verbally in partner or group tasks, occurring in approximately 70% of such opportunities. These behaviors occur specifically during verbal production tasks and academic oral language demands; Destiny does not exhibit these behaviors in informal peer conversation or non-academic verbal contexts. Function is assessed as escape from language production tasks that expose language deficits to peers.
Instructional and Environmental Conditions or Circumstances (describe the instructional and environmental conditions or circumstances that trigger/contribute to the pattern of behavior(s) being addressed):
Avoidance and withdrawal behaviors are most frequent under the following conditions: (1) During whole-class oral discussion or question-answer sessions where Destiny may be called on unexpectedly; (2) During partner or small-group reading activities where Destiny must read aloud to peers; (3) During oral presentations or when asked to share written work verbally with the class; (4) When called on to answer a question that requires extended verbal explanation rather than a brief factual response. Antecedent FBA data indicate that behaviors are absent or near zero during independent written tasks, visual projects, and informal peer conversation. Behaviors are significantly reduced in 1:1 settings with a familiar adult and in small-group pull-out instruction. Function of behaviors is assessed as escape from language production contexts in which Destiny fears peer judgment of her language errors.
Alternative Behaviors and Skills to be Taught and Monitored (describe the alternative behaviors and skills to be taught and monitored in order to address the pattern of behaviors and the instructional and environmental conditions/ circumstances previously described):
The following alternative behaviors and skills will be explicitly taught and monitored: (1) Prepared Response Strategy: Destiny will be given advance notice (written or verbal) of questions she will be asked before whole-class discussion, allowing her to formulate and rehearse a response. She will be explicitly taught to use this preparation time to write a brief response before speaking, reducing the language processing demand of spontaneous oral production. (2) Verbal Participation Gradient: Destiny will be taught that participation can take multiple forms—written response on whiteboard, thumbs up/down, partner discussion before whole group, or written response shared verbally with support. Staff will consistently offer these low-pressure entry points before escalating to cold-call verbal responses. (3) Self-Advocacy Script: Destiny will be explicitly taught and rehearsed a verbal or written phrase to use when she needs more time to formulate a response (e.g., “Can I have a moment to write my answer first?”). This script will be practiced with the SLP during pull-out sessions and reinforced by classroom staff. (4) Small-Group Practice as Bridge: Destiny will practice oral academic language responses in small-group pull-out before being expected to produce similar responses in whole-class settings, building confidence and schema through lower-stakes environments. (5) Partner Read Structure: During partner reading tasks, Destiny will be paired with a supportive peer who reads first; Destiny will echo-read or read a designated shorter section. This reduces the exposure risk of independent oral reading while maintaining participation.
Reinforcers of Desired Behaviors (describe how the alternative behaviors will be reinforced):
Desired behaviors will be reinforced using the following strategies, selected based on Destiny’s identified motivators (peer connection, music and creative projects, social recognition, and teacher approval): (1) Behavior-Specific Praise: Staff will provide immediate, private verbal or written praise when Destiny uses her self-advocacy script, volunteers a written response, or participates verbally in a small-group or partner setting (e.g., “Destiny, I noticed you shared your answer with your partner today — that took courage”). Praise will emphasize effort and risk-taking rather than language accuracy to reduce shame around language errors. (2) Participation Tracking Chart: Destiny will maintain a private weekly chart tracking verbal participation attempts (any form). Upon accumulating 10 participation marks in a week, she earns a preferred activity (e.g., music during independent work, a creative project choice, or participation in a class discussion topic she selects). (3) Competence-Building Visibility: The SLP and classroom teacher will regularly share examples of Destiny’s language growth with Destiny in private, framing progress in terms of skills gained rather than deficits. This addresses the shame and avoidance cycle underlying the BIP behaviors. (4) Low-Stakes Oral Success Experiences: Staff will deliberately create regular structured opportunities for Destiny to experience successful verbal academic participation in low-stakes settings (e.g., partner discussion, small group), building a history of positive oral language experiences that compete with the avoidance pattern.
Methods to Ensure Consistency of Implementation (describe the method(s) that will be used to ensure the consistency of the implementation of the positive behavioral interventions across the student’s school-sponsored instruction or activities (e.g., data collection, frequency, assessment of effectiveness of BIP, additional training for staff, etc.)):
The following methods will be used to ensure consistent implementation of the BIP across all school-sponsored instruction and activities: (1) Data Collection: The general education teacher and special education teacher will collect a daily tally of verbal participation attempts and avoidance behaviors (non-response, early exit requests, partner read refusal) during ELA and content-area classes. Data will be disaggregated by setting (whole class, small group, partner) to track generalization. Data will be reviewed weekly by the special education teacher and SLP. (2) Staff Communication and Training: All instructional staff who work with Destiny (general education teacher, special education teacher, SLP, content-area teachers) will receive a copy of the BIP and attend a 30-minute orientation prior to implementation. Training will include: how to use the Prepared Response and Participation Gradient strategies, avoiding cold-calling, and delivering praise that reinforces effort rather than accuracy. The BIP will be reviewed monthly during IEP team check-ins to assess fidelity. (3) Assessment of BIP Effectiveness: Participation tally and avoidance behavior data will be graphed monthly. The team will compare data to baseline to evaluate progress toward IEP Goal #4 (oral language/academic participation). If Destiny’s voluntary verbal participation does not increase within 6 weeks of consistent implementation, the team will convene to review the BIP and consider referral for additional anxiety evaluation or consultation with school counselor. (4) Parent Communication: Parents will receive a copy of the BIP and will be informed of Destiny’s progress through quarterly written reports. Staff will contact the family if avoidance behaviors increase significantly or if Destiny appears to be experiencing significant distress at school. Parent input on Destiny’s comfort with verbal communication at home will be solicited quarterly to inform school-based strategies.
